1. AI Opportunity & Ownership Lab

Understanding AI careers, human contributions, and data rights.

Full Explanation: The AI Shift

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is not just a trend; it is a fundamental shift in how work is done globally. For young Nigerians, this presents two paths: being left behind, or learning to use AI as a powerful tool for entrepreneurship and employment. Understanding AI means understanding that it is trained on human data. When you use AI, you are feeding it information.

Ownership and Copyright: Currently, the law in many places states that only humans can hold copyright. If an AI generates an image or text entirely on its own without significant human editing, you likely cannot claim legal ownership of it. However, if you write a detailed prompt and heavily edit the output, you are adding human creativity, which strengthens your claim. Always check the Terms of Service of the specific AI tool you are using.

2. Creator Rights & Protection Lab

Understanding copyright, platform risks, and content protection.

Full Explanation: Protecting Your Digital Assets

As a creator (musician, writer, artist, coder), the moment you put your original idea into a tangible format, you own the copyright. You do not need to register it with a government office for it to be yours. However, proving it is yours can be difficult if someone steals it. This is why adding watermarks, publishing dates, and keeping original project files is crucial.

Platform Risks: Social media platforms can issue strikes or ban accounts based on automated reports. If someone falsely reports your account, you need to know how to appeal. Furthermore, protecting your account with Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) is essential, as hackers frequently target creators to steal their audience.

3. Internet Affordability & Access Lab

Understanding the personal cost of internet access.

Full Explanation: The Digital Divide

Access to the internet is a basic requirement for modern education and business. However, the cost of data and smartphones creates a "digital divide," where poorer communities are locked out of opportunities. When a young person spends 20% of their income just to buy 2GB of data, they cannot afford to stream educational videos or attend online classes.

Low-Data Practices: You can fight back against high data costs by turning off auto-play videos, using "Lite" versions of apps (like Facebook Lite), and downloading files on public Wi-Fi networks instead of using your mobile data. Understanding your spending is the first step to managing it.

4. Youth Internet Governance Lab

Understanding digital rights and participating in policy.

Full Explanation: Who Makes the Rules?

Internet Governance is the process of deciding how the internet operates. This includes rules on data privacy, freedom of speech, and how much data companies can charge. Currently, these decisions are often made by older generations and large corporations. However, because young people are the primary users of the internet, their voices are essential.

Digital Rights: You have the right to privacy, the right to access information, and the right to express yourself safely online. By understanding these rights, you can advocate for better data protection laws and affordable internet access for students.

5. Cybersecurity & Online Safety Centre

Practical defense against scams and phishing.

Full Explanation: How Scams Work

Cybercriminals do not use magic; they use psychology. This is called "Social Engineering." A scammer will pretend to be your bank, a government official, or a friend in trouble. They create a sense of urgency (e.g., "Your account will be blocked in 1 hour!") to make you panic and click a bad link.

Phishing: This is when a scammer sends a fake email or text message that looks real, asking you to enter your password or BVN. Real banks will NEVER ask for your password or BVN via a text message link. If you are ever unsure, close the message and open your bank's official app directly.

6. Digital Skills Academy

Learning pathways for the modern digital economy.

Full Explanation: Beyond Just Using a Phone

Being able to scroll through TikTok or WhatsApp does not mean you have digital skills. True digital literacy means knowing how to use software to solve problems, create value, and protect yourself. The modern economy requires skills like data analysis, graphic design, coding, and digital marketing.

Digital Entrepreneurship: You can use the internet to start a business with zero physical shop. By learning how to build a simple website, use social media for marketing, and accept online payments, you become an employer rather than just a job seeker.

7. Youth Internet Observatory

Understanding youth experiences online.

Full Explanation: The Power of Data

Policymakers often ignore youth issues because there is no data to prove they exist. This observatory aims to collect anonymous data on what young Nigerians actually face online—whether it is cyberbullying, high data costs, or lack of privacy. By documenting these challenges, we can present hard evidence to the government and demand better policies.
