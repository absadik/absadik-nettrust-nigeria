Purpose

Mission, vision and objectives

A clear statement of what this project intends to do, and what it does not yet do.

Mission

To build a professional, mobile-first digital trust, AI opportunity, creator protection and internet governance platform focused on young Nigerians.

Vision

A safer, more inclusive and trustworthy internet in which young Nigerians can learn, create, work and participate in decisions that shape their digital lives.

Approach

A lightweight static website that works on low-end Android phones, avoids unnecessary dependencies, and is honest about what has and has not been built.

Objectives

  1. Explain AI opportunity and ownership in plain language for young Nigerians.
  2. Help creators understand basic rights, platform risk and where to report harm.
  3. Raise awareness of internet affordability and access barriers.
  4. Encourage meaningful youth participation in internet governance discussions.
  5. Share practical cybersecurity and online safety guidance.
  6. Organise digital skills learning pathways into one clear structure.
  7. Provide a structured space for youth internet observation and collaboration.

Status: objectives stated for the project. Learning content and tools are planned.

Discussion focus

Four NYIGF discussion themes

The project is organised around four discussion themes. These are framing topics for future content and conversation — they are not research findings, results or endorsements.

Theme 01

AI jobs and ownership

How AI tools are changing the work young people do, who owns the data and the outputs produced with those tools, and what young Nigerians need to know to take part fairly.

Theme 02

Creator rights and platform risks

The rights creators hold over their work, the risks of publishing on large platforms, and the practical steps creators can take when content is copied, misused or removed.

Theme 03

Internet affordability

The cost of data and devices, the quality of connections, and how affordability shapes who can learn, work and participate online.

Theme 04

Youth internet governance

How internet policy decisions are made, how young people can participate meaningfully, and how youth voices can be represented in governance processes.

Proposed approach

Proposed solutions

These are proposals for how the project intends to respond to the four discussion themes. They describe intended direction, not delivered results.

1

Plain-language digital trust education

Explain ownership, rights and safety in simple language and short formats that load quickly on low-end phones.

2

One organised platform instead of scattered information

Group related topics into eight clear modules so that a young user can find guidance without searching across many unrelated sources.

3

Lightweight, low-data design

No frameworks, no external APIs and no heavy media, so the site remains usable on slow connections and inexpensive devices.

4

Accessible by default

Readable typography, keyboard-friendly navigation, clear headings and touch-friendly controls from the very first version.

5

Honest labelling of unfinished work

Planned features are marked as planned. The project does not present unbuilt tools as working products.

6

Privacy-respecting foundation

No accounts, no forms, no tracking and no personal data collection in this stage of the project.
