1. AI Opportunity & Ownership

Please read the study notes carefully before taking the assessment.

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: Understanding Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks include learning, problem-solving, and decision-making. Tools like ChatGPT and Midjourney analyze vast amounts of existing data to predict and generate new text, images, or code.

It is important to understand that AI does not "think" creatively like a human. It operates on algorithms and patterns. When you give it a prompt, it calculates the most likely response based on its training data.

📘 STUDY NOTE 2: The Complex Issue of AI Ownership

Copyright law traditionally protects the original work of a human creator. If you ask an AI to generate an image or write an essay with minimal input, you generally cannot claim legal copyright ownership of that output, because a machine produced it.

However, human contribution is key. If you spend significant time writing detailed prompts, refining the output, and editing the final result (for example, using Photoshop to modify an AI-generated image), you are adding human authorship. This may give you a stronger claim to ownership, but it depends heavily on the specific AI tool's Terms of Service.

Practical Example: If you type "draw a house" and post the result, you likely do not own it. If you write a 3-paragraph prompt detailing the architectural style, lighting, and landscaping, and then manually correct errors in the image, you have contributed significant human effort.

2. Creator Rights & Protection

Please read the study notes carefully before taking the assessment.

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: Copyright Principles

Copyright is a legal right that grants the creator of an original work exclusive rights to its use and distribution. In Nigeria and internationally, this protection is automatic the moment your work is saved in a tangible format (like recording a song or saving a video file). You do not need to register it with the government to own it.

However, in the digital age, content theft is common. If someone downloads your video and reposts it without permission, they are infringing on your copyright. To prove ownership, you must keep your original project files (e.g., the raw footage, the .PSD file, or the original voice note).

📘 STUDY NOTE 2: Account Security and 2FA

Creators are frequent targets for hackers who want to steal their audience for scams. Using a simple or reused password makes you vulnerable. You must use Two-Factor Authentication (2FA).

2FA adds a second layer of security. Even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot log in without the unique code sent to your phone. It is the most effective way to protect your digital assets.

3. Internet Affordability

Please read the study notes carefully before taking the assessment.

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: The Digital Divide

Internet affordability is a major challenge in Nigeria. When a significant portion of your monthly income is spent on data, it creates a "digital divide." This limits access to online education, remote jobs, and digital business opportunities.

To manage this, you must practice data-efficient browsing. This means turning off video autoplay, downloading files when connected to free Wi-Fi, and using "Lite" versions of applications which consume significantly less data.

4. Youth Internet Governance

Please read the study notes carefully before taking the assessment.

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: Internet Governance

Internet Governance refers to the development and application of shared principles, norms, rules, and decision-making procedures that shape the evolution and use of the Internet. It involves governments, tech companies, and civil society.

Historically, youth have been excluded from these policy discussions. However, because young people are the primary users of the internet, their voices are essential. Advocating for digital rights, affordable data, and online safety ensures that policies reflect the reality of young Nigerians.

5. Cybersecurity & Safety

Please read the study notes carefully before taking the assessment.

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: The Mechanics of Phishing

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks. Phishing is one of the most common methods used by cybercriminals. They use deceptive emails or text messages that appear to come from legitimate sources (like your bank) to trick you into revealing sensitive information, such as passwords or your BVN.

They rely on social engineering—creating a false sense of urgency or fear (e.g., "Your account will be blocked today!"). Financial institutions will never request sensitive information via a text link. Always access your bank services directly through their official application.

6. Digital Skills

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: Digital Literacy

Passive consumption of social media content is not a digital skill. True digital literacy involves the ability to use technology to solve problems and create economic value. This includes proficiency in productivity software (like Google Workspace), graphic design (like Canva), and understanding how to market products online.

8. Innovation Hub

📘 STUDY NOTE 1: Design Thinking

Innovation begins with understanding a problem. The design thinking process involves: Empathizing (understanding the users), Defining the problem, Ideating (brainstorming solutions), Prototyping (building a simple version), and Testing. For example, creating a low-data educational platform is an innovative solution to the digital divide.
