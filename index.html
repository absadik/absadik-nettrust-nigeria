1. AI Opportunity & Ownership

Simple English Lesson

📖 Step 1: What is AI? (Think of a smart parrot) ▼

Imagine a parrot that has read every book in the world. If you ask it a question, it can answer you. But the parrot doesn't actually understand what it is saying—it just knows what words usually follow each other.

That is how Artificial Intelligence (AI) like ChatGPT works. It is a computer program that guesses the next word based on what it has read before.

⚠️ Step 2: Who owns what AI creates? ▼

If you ask AI to "Draw a dog," and it draws one, can you sell it and say you are the artist? The law says NO. Because a machine drew it, you cannot claim you own it. It is like taking a photo of someone else's painting.

But, if you tell the AI exactly how to draw the dog (e.g., "Draw a brown dog with a blue hat sitting under a mango tree"), and you use Photoshop to fix the dog's eyes... now you have added your own human work. You have a better chance of owning it!

2. Creator Rights & Protection

Simple English Lesson

📖 Step 1: Copyright is like baking a cake ▼

If you bake a cake, the cake belongs to you. You don't need to go to the government to register the cake. The same applies to your songs, photos, and videos. The moment you make it, it is yours.

If someone steals your cake and starts selling it, they are breaking the law. For creators, if someone downloads your video and posts it as their own, they are stealing your work.

🔒 Step 2: How to lock your account ▼

Hackers want to steal your account to scam your followers. If you use a simple password like "123456", they will guess it in seconds.

You must turn on 2FA (Two-Factor Authentication). This means even if a hacker gets your password, the app will send a secret code to your phone. Without that code, they cannot enter.

3. Internet Affordability

Simple English Lesson

📖 Step 1: Why is data so expensive? ▼

Imagine you get ₦1,000 as pocket money. If you spend ₦300 just to buy data, you only have ₦700 left for food. That is what many students face. This is called the Digital Divide—the gap between those who can afford the internet and those who cannot.

To save your money, use "Lite" apps (like Facebook Lite) which use less data. Turn off auto-play on videos, and download things when you are connected to free Wi-Fi.

4. Youth Internet Governance

Simple English Lesson

📖 Step 1: Who owns the Internet? ▼

Nobody owns the internet, but there are rules. "Internet Governance" is just a big word for "The Rules of the Internet."

Right now, older people in government and big companies are making the rules. But they are not the ones using TikTok or struggling with data costs! If young people don't speak up, the rules will never favor us.

5. Cybersecurity & Safety

Simple English Lesson

📖 Step 1: How scammers trick you ▼

Scammers are like robbers who wear a police uniform. They pretend to be your bank, or a friend, to get your password. They want you to panic (e.g., "Your account will be blocked today!").

Phishing is when they send you a fake link. If you click it, it looks like your bank's website, but it's a trap. If you enter your password, they will steal your money.
